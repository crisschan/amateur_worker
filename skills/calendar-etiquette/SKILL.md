## Calendar & Scheduling Best Practices

### Sending Meeting Invitations
- **Include an agenda** in the invite description — never send a blank invite.
- **Set a clear end time** — open-ended meetings overrun.
- **Invite only necessary participants.** For information only, send notes instead.
- Send invites at least **24 hours in advance** for standard meetings; **1 week** for large or external meetings.
- Attach any pre-read materials to the invite.

### Finding a Slot
- Check attendees' calendars for conflicts before proposing a time.
- Prefer **mid-morning (9–11am)** or **early afternoon (2–4pm)** slots.
- Avoid scheduling over lunch (12–1pm) unless it's a working lunch.
- Keep stand-ups to **15 minutes max**; decision meetings to **30–60 minutes**.

### Responding to Invites
- Accept, decline, or tentatively accept **within 24 hours**.
- If declining, propose an alternative time or explain in the response.
- Update your status if plans change — do not simply ignore the invite.

### During the Meeting
- Start on time; end on time or early.
- Assign a note-taker at the start.
- Park off-topic discussions in a "parking lot" for later.
- Capture action items with owners and due dates before ending.

### After the Meeting
- Distribute minutes or action items **within 24 hours**.
- Update the calendar event with any changed details or links.
- Follow up on overdue action items before the next meeting.

### Recurring Meetings
- Review the necessity of recurring meetings **quarterly**.
- Cancel or reschedule with at least **2 hours' notice** when possible.
- Update the recurring event rather than creating ad-hoc duplicates.
